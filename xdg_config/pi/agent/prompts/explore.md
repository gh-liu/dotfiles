---
description: Explore codebase context and constraints, then either surface clarifying gaps or propose distinct candidate options
argument-hint: "[problem, target, or focus]"
---

Investigate the codebase, docs, and discussion context for the task below to establish verified facts, constraints, and invariants—then either stop to clarify an ambiguous problem/root cause or generate distinct candidate options ready for comparison and decision.

Problem, target, or focus: ${@:-none}

1. **Ground in reality**: Before framing questions or proposing options, inspect the relevant code, entry points, callers, contracts, configuration, tests, and active ADRs (and check `git status`/diff if working on in-progress changes). When behavior depends on runtime state or external dependencies, perform read-only checks of the deployment or external environment (e.g., logs, live config, or dependency contracts) instead of guessing from source alone. For complex or cross-component flows, map the relevant control flow (CFG), data flow (DFG), call chains, and observable test seams (`seams`) where behavior can be isolated or verified independently. Extract concrete facts with `file:line` or observed command/log evidence, identifying existing invariants, hard constraints, and non-goals. If the request is solution-shaped without a stated goal, trace the affected code to reconstruct the underlying problem as a testable hypothesis rather than assuming the proposed mechanism is the only framing.
2. **Apply the stop gate**: Decide whether the problem space is clear enough to explore solution options.
   - **Stop (do not propose solution options)** if: (a) core requirements, acceptance criteria, non-goals, or high-impact constraints/invariants remain ambiguous after codebase inspection in a way that would materially change the solution space, interfaces, or architecture; or (b) the task is diagnostic/exploratory (explaining behavior or isolating a root cause) and no change goal or confirmed root cause is established yet. When stopping, surface only high-impact open questions (proposing defaults for reversible low-risk choices) or competing root-cause hypotheses paired with the smallest discriminating checks/experiments.
   - **Proceed to options** when the goal, acceptance criteria, and key boundaries are clear enough to compare concrete approaches—even if localized unknowns remain (track those explicitly as `unverified`).
3. **Explore distinct candidate options** *(only when the gate passes)*: Propose 2–4 genuinely distinct candidate options (differing in mechanism, boundary, or trade-off rather than cosmetic variants), including a minimal-change or status-quo baseline unless ruled out by hard constraints (if ruled out, note why briefly). Check every option against hard constraints, invariants, non-goals, and active ADRs. Describe each option in parallel, neutral terms (avoiding editorial adjectives) for fair comparison:
   - **Mechanism & scope**: How it achieves the goal and which files, modules, interfaces, or callers it touches.
   - **Verified facts**: Observed code, test, or doc evidence (`file:line`) backing feasibility or impact.
   - **Known risks, failure modes & reversibility**: Concrete counterexamples, invariant/regression risks, hidden coupling, and migration or rollback costs.
   - **Unverified assumptions & decisive checks**: Assumptions that could invalidate the option and the smallest check or experiment that would confirm or refute them.
4. **Report for decision**:
   - **Verified context**: Current behavior, key `file:line` references (plus observed runtime/environment evidence when checked), contracts, hard constraints, invariants, relevant active ADRs, and—for complex flows—key control/data paths (CFG/DFG) and test seams.
   - **Problem frame & gate outcome**: Restated goal, acceptance criteria, and non-goals, stating explicitly whether exploration **stopped for clarification/diagnosis** or **proceeded to candidate options**.
   - **Next-step payload**: If stopped, list the blocking questions (with proposed defaults for low-risk reversible choices) or competing hypotheses and discriminating checks. If proceeded, present the parallel candidate options, the decisive trade-offs separating them, and any high-leverage checks worth running before choosing (leaving final selection to the user).
5. **Boundaries**: Read-only exploration only (including any external or deployment environment checks); do not modify files, mutate remote/runtime state, leave artifacts in the working tree, implement any option, or perform destructive git/system actions. Do not invent unstated requirements, workloads, or thresholds, and clearly separate verified facts (with `file:line` or observed read-only command/log output) from inferences and unverified assumptions.
